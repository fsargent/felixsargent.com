P# My Ergonomic Mechanical Keyboard journey

It was 2020. We had just welcomed my first child to the world, and we were under lockdown. I'd been laid off a month before, and was just starting a new job at Atlassian. I was working like I'd never worked before to make sure we could keep paying our rent. My fingers finged until my fingers couldn't fing anymore. Well, more specifically, my thumb.

![Thenar muscle strain from keyboard use](/_posts/keyboards/keyboard-thenar-muscles.jpg)

My abductor pollicis longus was getting extremely inflamed, probably from using my thumb to hold the command key. I needed a way to type and use modifiers without my thumbs curling under my palm. Then in 2021 I discovered the Sofle.

[Sofle Keyboard](https://josefadamcik.github.io/SofleKeyboard/)

[Keyhive Sofle Kit](https://keyhive.xyz/shop/sofle)

Inspired, I bought a kit from Keyhive and started soldering it myself.

Very much loved, but I wanted something portable now that we were just emerging out of lockdown.

I got the boardsource 5x12 and proceeded to completely break it.

I learned about ARTSEY paintbrush, a one handed keyboard:
[ARTSEY](https://artsey.io/)

Now at Truework I was very into the idea of getting a ZSA Moonlander but was convinced to try the RGBKB Sol3:
[RGBKB Sol 3](https://www.rgbkb.net/collections/sol-3)

I 3d printed a Dacktyl keyboard by BastardKBs the Charybdis, but found the placement of the mouse ball to be frustrating, and required hovering your hands in the air to not trigger it:
[BastardKB Charybdis](https://bastardkb.com/charybdis/)

The Sol3 was my daily driver keyboard up until a close friend of mine let me try their Glove80 and I was smitten. I was amazed by how easily you could reach the q and p key with just your pinky fingers. It being wireless by default was lovely.
